# Shakespeare Authorship and Collaboration Disputes

This document serves as a testing checklist for the TruthVector Authorship Attribution module, detailing historical problems, disputes, and collaborations regarding William Shakespeare's works.

## Collaborations
Texts and authors to compare for collaborative attribution testing.

*   **John Fletcher**
    *   *Henry VIII* (also known as *All Is True*): Stylometric analysis often attributes specific scenes to Fletcher.
    *   *The Two Noble Kinsmen*: Co-authored with Fletcher, generally accepted in modern scholarship.
    *   *Cardenio* (Lost play): Likely co-authored with Fletcher, based on Cervantes' *Don Quixote*.
*   **Thomas Middleton**
    *   *Timon of Athens*: Strong linguistic and stylometric evidence suggests Middleton co-authored or heavily revised this play.
    *   *Macbeth*: Middleton is widely believed to have adapted/revised the surviving folio text (especially the Hecate scenes).
    *   *Measure for Measure*: Some evidence suggests later adaptation by Middleton.
*   **George Wilkins**
    *   *Pericles, Prince of Tyre*: Wilkins is highly favored as the co-author for the first two acts.
*   **Thomas Nashe**
    *   *Henry VI, Part 1*: Suspected co-author for Act 1.
*   **George Peele**
    *   *Titus Andronicus*: Often credited as co-author (especially Act 1).

## Disputed Plays
Plays of uncertain authorship where Shakespeare is a candidate. Test TruthVector's ability to confirm or reject Shakespearean presence.

*   *Edward III*: Increasingly included in modern Shakespeare canons; suspected co-authorship (possibly with Thomas Kyd or Christopher Marlowe). Test against early Shakespeare histories.
*   *Sir Thomas More*: A collaborative play by Anthony Munday and Henry Chettle. Shakespeare is believed to have contributed "Hand D" (a specific three-page manuscript revision). A critical test for fragment attribution.
*   *Love's Labour's Won*: A lost play. It is debated whether this is an alternative title for an existing play (e.g., *Much Ado About Nothing* or *The Taming of the Shrew*) or a completely destroyed sequel to *Love's Labour's Lost*.
*   *Double Falsehood*: Lewis Theobald's 18th-century play, which he claimed was based on Shakespeare and Fletcher's lost *Cardenio*.
*   *Arden of Faversham*: Sometimes attributed partially to Shakespeare, alongside Thomas Kyd.
*   *The Spanish Tragedy*: Later additions (the "Painter's Part" in the 1602 edition) are sometimes attributed to Shakespeare.

## Pseudonym Candidates
Major historical candidates proposed by Anti-Stratfordians as the "true" author of Shakespeare's works. Compare Shakespeare's canon against the known works of these candidates.

*   **Edward de Vere, 17th Earl of Oxford (Oxfordian Theory)**
    *   *Core Argument*: Parallels between his life and the plays (especially *Hamlet*); his acknowledged education, travel to Italy, and court connections.
    *   *Texts to Compare*: Oxford's surviving poetry, personal letters (to evaluate idiolect and vocabulary).
*   **Francis Bacon (Baconian Theory)**
    *   *Core Argument*: Bacon had the necessary legal, philosophical, and courtly knowledge; alleged ciphers hidden in the Folio texts.
    *   *Texts to Compare*: *Essays*, *The Advancement of Learning*, *New Atlantis*, surviving masques.
*   **Christopher Marlowe (Marlovian Theory)**
    *   *Core Argument*: Marlowe faked his 1593 death in Deptford to escape execution and continued writing under the Shakespeare pseudonym.
    *   *Texts to Compare*: *Doctor Faustus*, *Edward II*, *Tamburlaine*, *Hero and Leander*.
*   **William Stanley, 6th Earl of Derby (Derbyite Theory)**
    *   *Core Argument*: Traveled extensively, had a private theater troupe, and was reported by a contemporary to be "penning comedies for the common players."
    *   *Texts to Compare*: Surviving letters and minimal extant literary fragments.
